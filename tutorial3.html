Exporting Horizon Geometry And Textures
and bringing them into Unity for VRChat

(17 Step tutorial with full video coming soon)
CLICK A PICTURE TO ENLARGE IT.
Follow Along
Required: Tutorial 1 Optional: Tutorial 2

Step 1

This tutorial assumes you have already used either the standalone installer or the Meta Quest Link app for the Meta Horizon Worlds Desktop Editor at least once prior, and have already completed tutorial 1. Open the desktop editor of your choice, then navigate to/ open a world of your choice.

Step 2

The world will then load.

Step 3

Please remember when you export a horizon world, you're strictly exporting the geometry, materials and textures. You cannot export text, code, lighting, gizmos or color tints for use within Unity or VRChat Creator Companion. However we will have tutorials for those as well! Do not worry! We are gonna take this building and bring it over to Unity. The silver and black will stay but the red you see won't as it is a color tint.

Step 4

Open the top left hamburger menu and click "Export Geometry"

Step 5

Ensure you have export textures selected as you will more than likely not want to rebuild all of these.

Step 6

You can then name your FBX/world geometry and store it in an easily accessible folder of your choosing. I highly recommend a blank directory or a new folder.

Step 7

(Minus the tutorial pictures and folders at the bottom hahaha) you will see your exported textures and your FBX in your chosen directory when it is finished. Do not be alarmed, we do not have to rebuild any of these objects or texture materials in Unity, as the data for them is hardcoded in your Meta Horizon World Export. Thank you Meta!

Step 8

Now we need to open up the VRChat Creator Companion app so we can open up our project. If you do not have a desktop shortcut for this you can search for it in your Windows Search bar.

Step 9

Open up your chosen project by clicking Open Project to the right of your world. Again, this tutorial assumes you've already at least completed tutorial 1 and have a blank VRChat world in Unity, as well as potentially exploring Unity's UI and layout in tutorial 2.

Step 10

As you are aware if you have been following, this red runtime error is only because you may not have VRChat installed on your PC. This error does not affect us, and if you wish to you can clear it in the bottom left under the Project button.

Step 11

Then we wanna click the project tab and then the assets tab under the favorites area (bottom left). We are going to take the FBX from MHW and drag and drop that into Models, then we will do the same for the textures and place them into Textures. In Meta Horizon Worlds, materials are embedded, so we don't have to import, create or edit these.

Step 12

Open up your file explorer in a minimized view to where you exported your Horizon World, and drag and drop that into your Models folder in your VRChat world Assets Panel (the folder to the left of the highlighted scenes folder)

Step 13

Note that my file explorer is obscuring our textures folder so you may need to move the explorer around to drag and drop your textures into the Textures folder. but you want to drag all exported textures into your textures folder. Feel free to navigate to/click the textures folder after to see all your textures.

Step 14

Perfect, all our textures made it! Lets back out of the textures folder by clicking the Assets tab either above the textures/assets viewport or to the left where it sats Assets with multiple folders (bottom left.)

Step 15

Now lets navigate to our models folder and view our imported world geometry! Nice! look at all those ready to go materials.

Step 16

Now we need to drag and drop our world into our scene view. Then we want to reset the Unity transforms (position rotation scale) so that way it uses our exported Horizon transforms. This is important for preserving the size and placement of your prior Horizon World, and if that isn't important to you you can of course, skip this step.

Step 17

Look at that! Your exported Horizon World in Unity, in the same placement and position, same scale, with all its textures ready to go! Check out Tutorial 4 and Tutorial 5 to see how to create your own color tints, materials, and even fix the obvious background planes that are missing their transparencies. (See behind the building the black wall, which should be a transparent cutout.)
